About Kite

Growing up online, gently.

Kids deserve to grow into the internet, not get thrown into it at thirteen, or watched through it until they leave home.

Most parental controls were built to answer one question: what are they doing?

That sounds reasonable until you’re scrolling through a thousand lines of app names and timestamps on a Sunday night, trying to work out whether any of it matters.

The better question is how are they doing? Are they sleeping? Making things? Spending time with friends? Is something changing that’s worth a conversation?

Kite is built around that second question. It reads the firehose so you don’t have to, tells you what matters in a few sentences, and keeps your kid in on it the whole way. Then, year by year, it steps back.

What we believe.

Four ideas we come back to whenever we decide what Kite should, and shouldn’t, do.

  1. 1

    Summaries over surveillance

    You need to know how they’re doing, not read every message. Kite looks at patterns and tells you what matters.

  2. 2

    Kids are in on it

    Your kid knows Kite is there and, as they get older, sees the same summary you do. No secrets in either direction.

  3. 3

    Trust should grow

    A nine-year-old and a sixteen-year-old need different things. Kite loosens its grip a little every year.

  4. 4

    Your family isn’t the product

    We collect only what the summary needs, and we never sell or share what we learn about your kids.
